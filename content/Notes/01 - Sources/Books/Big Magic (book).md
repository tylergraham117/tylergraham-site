# Big Magic (book)



## Part 1: Courage

#### Hidden Treasures
- Liz tells the story of a poet named Jack Gilbert who taught in her position before her. He was a bit of a legend. 
	- "He told them that they must live their most creative lives as a means of fighting back against the ruthless furnace of this world." "Most of all, he asked his students to be brave. Without bravery, he instructed, they would never be able to realize the vaulting scope of their own capacities." (6)
		- What does is mean to be creatively brave? How do we practice it in our lives? 
	- "He smiled at the girl with infinite compassion and asked, 'Do you have the courage? Do you have the courage to bring forth this work? The treasures that are hidden inside you are hoping you will say yes.'" (7)
	- **This is the central question of creative living. 'Do you have the courage to bring forth the treasures within you?'**
#### Hidden Treasures
- "The universe buries strange jewels deep within us all, and then stands back to see if we can find them. The hunt to uncover those jewels -- that's creative living." (8)
	- **Creative living is the hunt to uncover the jewels buried deep inside.**
#### An Amplified Existence
- Creative living is about living a life that is **"driven more strongly by curiosity than fear."** (9)
- The beauty of returning to childhood hobbies as an adult is that we have a new perspective- one that allows us to "appreciate the value of (our) own joy." (11) As a kid, we don't know to notice it.
- Liz tells the story of her friend who returned to figure skating as an adult. She does it as a hobby because it makes her feel a transcendence she cannot access any other way. She wants to spend as much time as possible in that state. That's creative living.
	- A creative life is an amplified life. It's a bigger, happier, expanded, more interesting life.
#### Scary, Scary, Scary
- "And we all know that **when courage dies, creativity dies with it.** We all know that fear is a desolate boneyard where our dreams go to desiccate in the hot sun." (13)
- There is a long list of valid reasons to be scared. It's all scary. But letting that fear exist unchecked within us is how our dreams die. 
#### Defending Your Weakness
- **"Argue for your limitations and you get to keep them."** (18)
	- Why do we do this? When we make excuses and argue for our limits, we end up stuck with them. There's no reason to do this. Why would we want to keep our limitations? This is where we need courage and bravery to live beyond our limitations.
#### Fear Is Boring
- Living in **fear is a boring way to live.** It's the same thing every day. Everyone has fear, it's not unique. If we want to live a amplified existence, we need to look for something else. Something interesting and creative and exciting. 
#### The Fear You Need and the Fear You Don't Need
- You do not need to become fearless in order to live a creative life.
	- "Bravery means doing something scary. Fearlessness means not even understanding what the word scary means." (22)
- **"Your fear will always be triggered by your creativity, because creativity asks you to enter into realms of uncertain outcome, and fear hates uncertain outcome."** (23)
	- Fear shows up strongest when there's ambiguity. Expect it.
#### The Road Trip
- The fear we feel when we interact with creativity will never go away. It does not recede with experience. It is part of the creative process. It's a signal you're heading towards uncertainty. Luckily, that means you're probably onto something.
	- Fear can be a signal you're heading towards something creative. 
- "This is why we have to be careful of how we handle our fear - because I've noticed that when people try to kill off their fear, they often end up inadvertently murdering their creativity in the process. So I don't try to kill off my fear. I don't go to war against it. Instead, I make all that space for it. Heaps of space. Every single day." (24)
	- Rather than fighting against fear, make space for it. It'll be there either way.
	- **"In fact, I cordially invite fear to come along with me everywhere I go. I even have a welcoming speech prepared for fear, which I deliver right before embarking upon any new project or big adventure."** (25)
		- I love this idea. I should have a welcoming speech for big emotions like this, including fear. 
#### Why It's Worth It
- Carrying your fear around isn't comfortable or easy. 
- "If you can't learn to travel comfortably alongside your fear, you'll never be able to go anywhere interesting or do anything interesting." (26)
- "And that would be a pity, because life is short and rare and amazing and miraculous, and you want to do really interesting things and make really interesting things while you're still here" (27)
- **We all have treasures hidden within us, and "bringing those treasures to light takes work and faith and focus and courage and hours devotion, and the clock is ticking, and the world is spinning, and we simply do not have time anymore to think so small.**" (27)
## Part 2: Enchantment
#### An Idea Arrives
- Inspiration is the feeling of an idea coming to you.
- It can be subtle or it can be intense.
#### How Ideas Work
- Ideas are their own entities. They have no material body, but they do have consciousness. They are driven by one impulse: to be made manifest. And an idea can only be made manifest in our world through collaboration with a human partner.
- **"When an idea thinks it has found somebody - say, you - who might be able to bring it into the world, the idea will pay you a visit. It will try to get your attention. Mostly, you will not notice. This is likely because you're so consumed by your own dramas, anxieties, distractions, insecurities, and duties that you aren't receptive to inspiration. You might miss the signal because you're watching TV, or shopping, or brooding over how angry you ar eat somebody, or pondering your failures and mistakes, or just generally really busy."** (35)
	- How do we allow ourselves to be more open to ideas? Establishing a time of openness to them each day is probably a good start. In other words, be consistent with your creative work, and sometimes your schedule will overlap with inspiration's.
#### What Happens When You Say Yes
- "You have officially entered into a contract with inspiration, and you must try to see it through, all the way to its impossible-to-predict outcome." (38)
#### An Idea Goes Away
- **If an idea is left alone for too long, it will leave.** They want to be made manifest, so they will carry on to another human collaborator. **There is no saving ideas for later.** If you must leave an idea alone for a bit, you must at the very least check in frequently.  **You are under agreement with inspiration, best not to break it.**
#### Multiple Discoveries
- "'When the time is ripe for certain things, they appear at different places, in the manner of violets coming to light in early spring.'" - János Bolyai
- This whole section speaks to the feeling many artists encounter where they have an idea, and then see it made manifest by someone else. Many take this as a form of theft, but it is not that, of course.
- "In the end, **it's all just violets trying to come to light.** Don't fret about the irrationality and unpredictability of all this strangeness. Give into it. Such is the bizarre, unearthly contract of creative living. There is no theft; there is no ownership; there is no tragedy; there is no problem. There is no time or space where inspiration comes from - and also no competition, no ego, no limitations. **There is only the stubbornness of the idea itself, refusing to stop searching until it has found an equally stubborn collaborator.** (Or multiple collaborators, as the case may be.) **Work with that stubbornness. Work with it as openly and trustingly and diligently as you can. Work with all your heart, because - I promise - if you show up for your work day after day after day after day, you just might get lucky enough some random morning to burst right into bloom.**" (63)
	- A good idea will be stubborn in its need to be realized. If we match that stubbornness, we might get lucky.
#### Hard Labor vs. Fairy Dust
- "In ancient Greek, the word for the highest degree of human happiness is *eudaimonia*, which basically means 'well daemoned' - that is, nicely taken care of by some external divine creative spirit." (67)
- "the Romans didn't believe that an exceptionally gifted person *was* a genius, they believed that an exceptionally gifted person *had* a genius." (67)
- This is a helpful mindset switch. It takes the pressure off. Rather than trying to be this perfect version of yourself, you're simply interacting with your guide.
#### Pinned Beneath the Boulder
- Liz tells the story of Harper Lee, author of *To Kill a Mockingbird.*
	- She never wrote another book after that. 
- When artists reach large commercial success, there is a huge pressure that comes along. Will they ever be able to make work that successful again?
	- That isn't up to them.
	- The idea of 'topping' that success assumes that there is a 'top.' 
		- Creativity isn't all about commercial success or winning some sort of competition. The antidote to this is reframing it. Ask instead: why do I live a create life? Why do I create?
- After a large success, it might be useful to immediately start a different, unrelated project of a different category. (She suggests that Harper Lee should've made a few easy books, like a romance, a children's story, a cookbook, anything.)
	- The idea is to lower the stakes.
- **"Just because creativity is mystical doesn't mean it shouldn't also be demystified - especially if it means liberating artists from the confines of their own grandiosity, panic, and ego."** (72)
	- Creative people build up their work in their minds way too much and it traps them.
#### Let It Come and Go
- **Your genius will come and go on its own schedule. Because of that, we have to graciously and consistently show up for our work, and leave the door open in case our genius decides to make an appearance. Assisted or not, we must be grateful for the opportunity to do the creative work.**
## Part 3: Permission
#### Your Permission Slip
- "Human beings have been creative beings for a really long time - long enough and consistently enough that it appears to be a natural impulse. To put the story in perspective, consider this fact: The earliest evidence of recognizable human art is forty thousand years old. The earliest evidence of human agriculture, by contrast, is only ten thousand years old. Which means that somewhere in our collective evolutionary story, **we decided it was way more important to make attractive, superfluous items that it was to learn how to regularly feed ourselves."** (87)
	- Art communicates ideas, and in a world where we have no written record, art becomes an important way to communicate with the past and future.
- Being creative is your birthright.
	- "It's your birthright as a human being, so do it with a cheerful heart." (88)
		- Why do we make such a big deal out of making stuff now? Commerce?
- "To call someone 'a creative person' is almost laughably redundant; creativity is the hallmark of our species." (89)
	- **Creativity is one of the defining characteristics of humanity.**
- **"The guardians of high culture will try to convince you that the arts belong only to a chosen few, but they are are wrong and they are also annoying. We are *all* the chosen few. We are all makers by design."** (89)
- Creativity is a fundamental part of humanity. **We don't need permission to make, and we don't need to make a big deal out of it. We can just make stuff.**
#### Decorate Yourself
- We are only on this earth for a short while. Why would we be so precious and scared to decorate?
- "I don't want to be afraid of bright colors, or new sounds, or big love, or risky decisions, or strange experiences, or weird endeavors, or sudden changes, or even failure." (91)
	- **It's all temporary. Why be so precious about it? Why not create with whimsy? That's what lights us up.**
#### Entitlement
- "Creative entitlement doesn't mean behaving like a princess, or acting as though the world owes you anything whatsoever. No, creative entitlement simply means believing that *you are allowed to be here*, and that - merely by being here - you are allowed to have a voice and a vision of your own." (92)
	- **Cultivate a sense of creative entitlement. You belong and your vision does too.**
	- Creative entitlement is the belief that we have an innate right to exist and have a voice and a vision and use that to shape the world around us.
- Poet David Whyte calls it "the arrogance of belonging"
- Most art just wants to say "I am here." You have the right to say that.
- **"Defending yourself as a creative person beings with defining yourself. It begins when you declare your intent."** (94)
	- Claim what you are. Stand up tall and be brave.
- "This proclamation of intent and entitlement is not something you can do just once and then expect miracles; **it's something you must do daily, forever**." (95)
	- We have to claim our creative intent every day.
	- We must constantly remind ourselves of our creative entitlement and intent to do something with it.
- Affirming your desire to live a creative life should be taken very seriously.
- "'Who the hell do you think you are?' your darkest interior voices will demand. 'Its funny you should ask', you can reply, 'I'll tell you who I am:'" (96)
	- **Have an answer to the question 'who do you think you are?'**
#### Originality vs. Authenticity
- "Attempts at originality can often feel forced and precious, but authenticity has a quiet resonance that never fails to stir me." (98)
	- **The drive for originality comes from fear.** We're afraid of copying, or stealing, of looking less than creative. Shoot for what feels good to share. Make sure it comes from you.
	- **Authenticity is more interesting than originality because it's human and real.**
#### Motives
- "**You are not required to save the world with your creativity.** Your art not only doesn't have to original, in other words; **it also doesn't have to be important**. For example: Whenever anybody tells me they want to write a book in order to help other people, I always think, *Oh, please don't.* Please don't try to help me. I mean, it is very kind of you to want to help people, but please don't make it your solve creative motive, because **we will feel the weight of your heavy intention, and it will put a strain upon our souls**." (98)
	- Don't make art with the sole intention of making something important and helpful to other people. It will weigh heavy on the soul.
- British columnist Katherine Whitehorn: [["You can recognize the people who live for others by the haunted look on the faces of others."]] (99)
- **Don't make art to help people. Make it to help yourself. To work through something. To have something beautiful. To sort your thoughts. Anything. Then give it away.** 
- Writing helps clarify your thoughts. Teaching is the best way to learn. Writing about a subject you are fascinated with is enjoyable and useful. It doesn't need to be for an audience. The writing is the thing that will benefit you.
- We can use selfish motives to do unselfish things. Make art for yourself, and it will surely help others on accident.
- "Your own reasons to create are reason enough. Merely by pursuing what you love, you may inadvertently end up helping us plenty. ([["There is no love which does not become help"]], taught the theologian Paul Tillich.) Do whatever brings you to life, then. **Follow your own fascinations, obsessions, and compulsions. Trust them. Create whatever causes a revolution in your heart.**" (101)
	- Do what brings you life. Follow your fascinations. Create what causes a revolution in your heart.
#### The Fat Kids
- **If the desire is to live a creative life forever, then it is far too early to be panicking about running out of time.**
- It seems like Liz has never been timid about sharing her work. This is a huge departure from me. She's really internalized the creative entitlement. How can I cultivate a similar mentality? *Probably through practicing it.*
- Anytime she sent out her work and received a no in return, she would immediately send out another query. 
	- "I decided to play the game of rejection letters as if it were a great cosmic tennis match: **Somebody would send me a rejection, and I would knock it right back over the net**, sending out another query that same afternoon. My policy was: *You hit it to me, I'm going to hit it straight back out into the universe.*" (112)
- If you get a no, respond immediately. Keep the momentum going.
- **Art should be prosocial.** Get a group of carefully selected people together that have similar goals. Establish real community. Keep each other to deadlines and encourage each other.
	- It should also be fun and not a business meeting.
#### Werner Herzog Chimes In
- Nobody asked you to do this. You are entitled to create, but people do not have to enjoy it or pay for it. 
	- "'Quit your complaining. It's not the world's fault that you wanted to be an artist. It's not the world's job to enjoy the films you make, and it's certainly not the world's obligation to pay for your dreams. Nobody wants to hear it. Steal a camera if you must, but stop whining and get back to work.'" (115, Werner Herzog)
#### A Trick
- **"Every artist complains, so it's a dead and boring topic."** (117)
	- **Complaining only harms you and bores others.**
- Creating things is hard. That should not be surprising. Complaints come when it's harder than expected. But *of course* it's hard to create. If it was easy, it wouldn't be special or interesting.
- "nobody ever really listens to anybody else's complaints" "we're all too focused on our own holy struggle, so basically you're talking to a brick wall." (117)
	- Nobody is really listening to your complaints. Why waste your words?
- **Complaining scares off inspiration. Why would it come to you if you're no fun to be around?**
- Instead of complaining about the work being hard, tell yourself *you enjoy the work*.
	- Tell yourself 'I enjoy my creativity'
- "I believe that **enjoying your work with all your heart is the only truly subversive position left** to take as a creative person these days. It's such a gangster move, because hardly anybody ever dares to speak of creative enjoyment aloud, for fear of not being taken seriously as an artist. So say it. Be the weirdo who dares to enjoy." (118)
#### Pigeonholing
- "Somebody said to me the other day, 'You claim that we can all be creative, but aren't there huge differences between people's innate talents and abilities? Sure, we can all make some kind of art, but only a few of us can be great, right?' I don't know. Honestly, you guys, I don't even really care." (119)
	- People spend so much time and energy on weird semantic arguments, building boxes for others in their minds. Ignore this trap. It's a waste of time. *Who cares.* This is not useful. 
-  "I beg you not to worry about such definitions and distinctions, then, okay? It will only weigh you down and trouble your mind, and we need you to **stay as light and unburdened as possible in order to keep you creating**." (120)
- People love to create boxes that don't exist. [[01 - Sources/Books/Why Fish Don't Exist (book)|Why Fish Don't Exist (book)]]
